Background

From bare metal to the cluster, no layers skipped.

I started installing physical servers and wiring networks. Then came Linux systems, infrastructure as code, the cloud — AWS above all — and finally Kubernetes. I did not arrive at the cloud from development: I came up from the bottom, one layer at a time.

That does not make me a specialist in any one of them, and I do not claim to be. It makes me useful when a problem spans several at once — which is most of the time. A service that stops responding is rarely explained by looking at a single layer.

Today I work as a DevOps Engineer at GFT Technologies, on an LLM conversational service on AWS for BBVA. Before that I spent nine years at Paradigma Digital building and running cloud infrastructure for enterprise clients.

In parallel, I teach. I prepare professionals for CKA, CKAD and CKS, and I build the labs they practise on myself. It is the part I enjoy most: it forces you to understand things properly, because a student always asks exactly what you had not looked at.
